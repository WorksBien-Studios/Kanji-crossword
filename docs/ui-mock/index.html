漢字ナンクロ 広告なし · UI redesign

A puzzle app that looks like the paper magazine its players grew up with

I read the app, its store research and the shipped puzzle data, worked out who plays and what makes them quit, then rebuilt the screens on the same iOS 18 shell (遊ぶ / 記録 / 設定). The phone on the right is live. Tap the board, the tiles and the tabs, or jump to any state with the chips.

Who plays this

The daily solver, 60–90
Plays at a fixed time of day, often with reading glasses, often one-handed. Grew up on puzzle magazines and pencils. Reviews mention a 92-year-old who plays every day and a daughter playing with her mother. Text size and calm matter more here than features.
The brain-trainer, 40–60
Wants puzzles that are actually hard, a personal best and a sense of progress. Reviews ask for completion time to be scored and for harder top-end puzzles.
The pair
Couples solve together and pass the phone back and forth. The board has to read at arm's length, and one person's wrong guess must never wreck the other's progress.
The ad refugee
Loved a competitor, then left over long ads after every puzzle. One reviewer says they would pay about ¥1,000 to remove them. Another calls a rival "20% quiz, 80% advertising". Anything that smells like monetisation is a reason to leave.

What appeals, and what doesn't

Green tags come from the store reviews quoted in the README. Grey tags are my inference from the age and habits of the audience.

Appeals

  • Big, high-contrast text and generous tap areas reviews
  • The finished grid staying on screen reviews
  • Everyday vocabulary and a one-tap reading for each word reviews
  • Timer only when asked for, plus a personal best reviews
  • Controls with words on them, in plain Japanese inferred
  • A print-like board: ink on paper, Mincho kanji inferred
  • A daily ritual: 今日の一問, the date in 令和, a stamp on the calendar inferred
  • Hints that explain themselves inferred

Doesn't appeal

  • Ads, forced video, redirects to the App Store reviews
  • Small text, grey-on-grey captions reviews
  • Screens that change on their own after finishing reviews
  • A valid answer being rejected, or puzzles that are too easy reviews
  • Icon-only toolbars and the hidden ⋯ menu inferred
  • Streak counters, lives, coins, confetti inferred
  • Drag or swipe as the only way to do something inferred
  • A sales pitch while a puzzle is open inferred

Found in the puzzle data

Only 87 of the 360 puzzles repeat a number

In the other 273, every number sits in exactly one cell and words share cells where they cross. For those, the promised "tap a number and every matching square lights up" does nothing, and the tutorial's step 3 ("同じ番号にまとめて入ります") does not match what happens. The mock handles both cases. A repeated number lights all its cells yellow, and the words a cell belongs to appear in a strip above the board. The demo puzzle is one of the 87, so you can see both.

Decision for you: keep the mixed library and reword the tutorial, or regenerate so every puzzle is a true ナンクロ.

Build notes

Everything here stays inside the shell's rules: the same three Tabs, NavigationStack, system sheets with detents, no third-party UI. It needs a small theme layer (colour, tint and font tokens, a ButtonStyle for tiles and the action bar) and .toolbarVisibility(.hidden, for: .tabBar) on the board. This is a mock in HTML, so spacing is faithful but the iOS chrome is drawn, not rendered by UIKit. I could not open the ios-18-shell repo from this session, so the chrome follows how the app uses it.
